### 2026/1792 — Nonlinear subspace trails for Gröbner attacks on Poseidon/Poseidon2/Neptune (Li, Liu, Wang) · axis 2 · posted 08-24
- **Claims [READ]:** a "parametric Macaulay matrix method" that turns the search for degree-suppressing constraints into a parametric system; for CICO with `Ec` extra constraints, "the nonlinear subspace trail can cover up to `2Ec` internal rounds, whereas the previous linear subspace trail can cover up to `Ec` rounds"; "for the Poseidon/Poseidon2 and Neptune instances proposed by Grassi et al. in ToSC 2025 … under the same complexity bound and the same Gröbner basis cost model, the nonlinear subspace model can analyze approximately twice as many internal partial rounds"; "for several concrete instances, our method reaches or even exceeds the recommended number of internal rounds given by the designers in sponge mode or compression mode."
- **Bears on:** VERDICTS §5h and `notes/gsr-poseidon-2026-1692.md` §3, whose lineage table has the row "Grassi–Koschatko–Rechberger 2025: + some partial rounds, needs invariant subspace" — 1792 is the direct successor of that row (same Gröbner+trail lineage, different from GSR's affine-gadget lineage). Our deployed instance is BabyBear `t=16, α=7, R_F=8, R_P=13` (§5h [OURS]). §5h already holds that the internal layer is absorbable in full by GSR and that "α=7 is what saves us"; the AXIS claim in `notes/formal-cryptanalysis-pipeline.md` §1 says R_F is load-bearing, not R_P. A second lineage independently reaching "R_P is exceeded" is *consistent* with that. What could move: (a) whether the nonlinear trail also eats initial full rounds beyond GSR's one — that is the 3-round ceiling; (b) whether it gains at CICO-k for k at the capacity (8), where §5h says GSR "gives nothing" and where the ~124-bit sponge claim lives. Unknown from the abstract whether our exact `(p,t,α)` is among the ToSC 2025 instances.
- **[READ, PDF `mirror/2026/1792.pdf`]** — (i) `Ec` is exactly the CICO degrees-of-freedom count: the paper restates [29]'s budget as *"Ec = r − min{c, d}"* (§1.3) and, in its own concrete instance, *"two input coordinates and two output coordinates are constrained, the available extra constraint budget is Ec = 16 − 2 − 2 = 12 … a nonlinear subspace trail of length 2Ec = 24"* (§3, KoalaBear `t=16, α=3`, "not a complete attack, but an explicit nonlinear subspace trail") — so hash-delta's identification `Ec ≡ t − (c+d)` **is confirmed at source**. (ii) The cost tables never visit a 31-bit prime: Table C.1 (p. 48, sponge mode, 2^128 budget, `rF = 6`, no margin) runs `p ≈ 2^64 … 2^256` with `c = d = ⌈log_p 2^{2κ}⌉`; Table C.2 (p. 49, compression mode, `c = 0`) lists the instances that reach the designers' `rP`: `p≈2^64, t=24, d=4, α=3` (nonlinear 41 vs baseline 39 / with-margin 42) and `p≈2^256, t∈{22,24}, d=1, α=7` (43/47 vs 43/47). (iii) The paper's "designers' recommendation" for the sponge is `rF = 6` plus a margin of *"two extra external full rounds and 7.5% extra internal partial rounds. Thus, rF = 6 is increased to rF = 8"* (§2.1). **For our Merkle node** (`t=16, c=0, d=8, p≈2^31, α=7, R_F=8, R_P=13`) this gives [DERIVED from (i)] `Ec = 8`, nonlinear window `2Ec = 16 ≥ R_P = 13` — the structural fact hash-delta derived — **and no cost**: every table entry is at `p ≥ 2^64` and `d ≤ 4`, the residual after linearization is all eight full rounds at α=7, and the paper computes nothing at our prime. The sentence at risk therefore stays a question, not a finding.
- **Owned by `hash-delta-2026-09-04.md` §1.2** — its derived table (Merkle node `c=0,d=8`: GKR25 linear window 8, 1792 claims up to 16, vs `R_P=13`; leaf sponge window 0) is the sharper statement of the same bearing and supersedes the paragraph above on *which instance* is exposed: the **Merkle compression node**, not the sponge. Whether 1792's `E_c` is GKR25's `t−(c+d)` is the first thing its PDF must settle.

### 2026/1760 — Midpoint Reset: full-round Poseidon collision from an adaptively chosen MDS (Jo) · axis 2 · posted 08-21
- **Claims [READ]:** "an explicit compression collision for all 28 rounds of the KoalaBear Poseidon instance with parameters (t,α,R_F,R_P)=(16,3,8,20), in the setting where the round constants are fixed before the MDS linear layer is chosen"; constructs a linear layer that cancels the midpoint against the next constant so "the following odd cubic S-box receives opposite states"; "the result exposes an adaptive correlation between fixed round constants and a subsequently chosen linear layer that matrix-only checks do not capture."
- **Bears on:** the parameter set is the Plonky3 KoalaBear set VERDICTS §1b calls "recommended, unexecuted" — but the threat model is a *designer* choosing M after the constants. Deployed Plonky3 layers are structural (`M_E = P_{t/4} ⊗ M_4`, `poseidon2-audit-verdict.md` [READ p3-poseidon2/src/external.rs:135-159]; `M_I` diagonal+ones), not fitted to constants, so no deployed instance is reached. Where it *is* ours: `notes/ring-hash-design.md` §1.3 item 2 "Program the dense layer as a Cauchy matrix, don't sample it" together with round constants that are "borrowed (a width-~12 prime-field Poseidon set)" (`ring-hash-design.md:438, :588`) — i.e. constants fixed first, matrix chosen by us afterwards, which is exactly this paper's setting. Twill is not exposed: `weft-c-spec.md` §1f derives constants by cSHAKE256 from a versioned tag and the mixing layer is the structural additive-NTT transform. Matrix-only invariant-subspace checks (`ring-hash-design.md:534`: "V is invariant only when both the coefficients and the round constants lie in F_q") are the class the paper says is insufficient.
- `hash-delta-2026-09-04.md` row 2 adds [READ there] that it was *submitted to the Poseidon Initiative 31 July 2026*, one day before the Initiative's unexplained pause, and that it is the first published full-round Poseidon collision of any kind — under an adversarial-matrix rule.
- **[READ, PDF `mirror/2026/1760.pdf`, pp. 1, 6–7]** — the setting is the Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative's collision challenge: *"the round constants are fixed while the attacker may provide an MDS matrix satisfying the prescribed linear-layer checks. The stated rules did not explicitly prohibit choosing the matrix as a function of those constants"* (§1). The construction is "midpoint reset": prescribe `M·u_r = −c^{(r+1)}` so the next odd S-box sees opposite inputs; the matrix is determined by `L+2 ≤ t` prescribed images (`L=14, t=16`, §6). The matrix "is MDS and passes the Initiative's linear-layer checks against the infinitely long subspace trails" (§1, §5: `verify_mds_matrix(M,p)=True` at the pinned commit). §6 states the countermeasure in our terms: *"derive the complete parameter set non-adaptively, for example from a common seed or by binding the round constants to a canonical encoding of the matrix."* §6 also places it beside Slipway (2026/1579, RC-dependent MDS) and the Bhati–Tariq–Ashur "From Round Skipping to S-Box Skipping" attack (fixed linear layer, restricted partial-round S-box inputs) — which its bibliography identifies as **eprint 2026/1692, i.e. GSR**, the paper VERDICTS §5h already applies to our deployed instance in full. So 1760's own placement says: the adaptive-matrix collision and GSR are complementary degrees of freedom, and the one that reaches fixed public instances is the one we have already priced.
- **Action: NOTE → design rule for 08-ATTACK-BRIEFS BRIEF 1**: derive the Cauchy points and the round constants from one XOF stream, constants *after* the matrix, and add "constant/matrix correlation" to the brief's checklist. Not verdict-changing.
