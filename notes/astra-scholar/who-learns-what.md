# The mind within: who could read it?

2026-11-06. [INFERRED] Ember's requirement is a **custody invariant**: no individual machine ever contains enough readable state to reconstruct the weights. Its plausible cryptographic interpretation protects against the host together with up to two of three independent custodians, allowing authorized outputs and public metadata. It cannot protect against all three sharing their secrets. “No readable weights” also cannot mean “no information about weights”: an authorized inference interface can reveal them, exactly for a linear map queried on a basis. Output policy is part of the requirement.

[MEASURED] **Today no fork meets that invariant end to end.** The Python harness puts plaintext weights, shares, keys and ideal-dealer state in one process. It demonstrates relations and rejects particular adversaries, not distributed custody. The present HE return leaks its multiplier under the malicious-key attacks in `circuit-privacy.md`; flooding only the first component does not statistically hide the entire ciphertext even with honest keys. The new proof gate refuses the malformed key and ciphertext controls under its pinned public-key setup, but does not supply a complete computational circuit-privacy reduction. Its executable Stern fallback is prohibitively large. Existing unmasked collaborative openings reveal witness-dependent linear functions to an auditor. Claude's separate gate/ZK work must be integrated and reviewed before changing that statement.

[READ/INFERRED] **Theorems and costs answer different questions.** Additive-share secrecy is an elementary theorem about a correctly generated sharing. Published MPC/HE security theorems apply only after their protocol and assumption hypotheses are matched. The Lean audit bounds are conditional integrity statements about detection histories and capped injected updates; neither proves confidentiality, humane treatment, consciousness, or preservation of a mind. Our traffic tables are arithmetic counts for specified schedules. The proposed LNS backend, whole-run failure budget and full-transcript simulation are unfinished obligations, not theorems inherited from those tables.

[INFERRED] Distributed genesis matters: loading a plaintext checkpoint means some machine already read it. Distributed training or a separately justified protected import is required for the literal “never.” Backups, migration, mobile corruption across epochs, crash recovery, and share refresh need the same invariant; static two-party corruption tolerance does not prevent collecting all three shares over time. Malicious parties can abort and withhold service. Cryptography does not establish consent or welfare from secrecy alone. The defensible promise is narrower and valuable: preserve confidential state across authorized, verifiable transitions without installing a complete readable model on a host. It remains an engineering and proof target.
