## The distinction that changes the construction

[DERIVED] Binding a randomness commitment establishes which declared object the receipt names. It does not establish that the committed value was honestly sampled, hidden before selection, uniquely assigned to a semantic transition, or selected for release without bias. Those are five separate claims. A verifier proving `r = PRF(seed, context)` only enforces a deterministic rule; it does not establish the seed distribution or prevent choosing among contexts after observing results.

[SOURCE: game read] Datta–Guan–Korb–Sahai, *“(Multi-Input) FE for Randomized Functionalities, Revisited”*, local ePrint `2025/330.pdf`, Definition 4.5, printed pp.24–25, defines malicious-encryptor security by comparing decryption to extraction plus ideal sampling. The output register is indexed by stored function-key index and exact ciphertext tuple. Repeating that tuple reuses the recorded output; a fresh tuple causes fresh ideal coins. The extractor receives the master secret. This is a primitive game, not a no-master-read or release-finality construction.

[DERIVED from that game] Reencrypting the same semantic observation to a different valid ciphertext tuple supplies a distinct ideal sampling opportunity. Selecting a favorable sample is allowed in the ideal world itself. Therefore even a perfect realization of Definition 4.5 cannot, by that theorem alone, establish an unbiased distribution among outputs a host later chooses to finalize or deliver. It can still protect the complete oracle interaction exactly as defined.

[SOURCE: construction and reduction read] The same paper §5.4, printed pp.47–48, constructs a valid PRF family whose maliciously supplied key selects a hardcoded output mode; its accompanying NIZK proves encryption consistency without proving honest PRF-key sampling. Theorem 6.9, pp.67–70, places output randomness under a fresh PRF key in the function-key program and compares the oracle to ideal uniform outputs. Its transcript caches exact tuples. I inspected that theorem's hybrid sequence, not every earlier malicious-decryptor reduction.

[SOURCE: cumulative-key game read] Definition 4.4, printed p.23, conditions malicious-decryptor challenge success on randomized compatibility with the entire set of the adversary's `KeyGen` queries. Definition 4.3, pp.22–23, quantifies every key in that set and substitutions in corrupted input slots. The displayed games on pp.22–25 contain no operation removing previously received keys or their outputs. This observation is scoped to those inspected games; it does not assert an absence of revocable rFE literature.

[OPEN] A compact mechanism that supplies inaccessible state coins under the resident's tier-A/B exposures remains a separate construction obligation. A public beacon may constrain timing or unpredictability before publication; it does not provide secret development entropy once its value is public. A private PRF seed gives pseudorandom continuation only while the permitted exposure does not reveal it.

[DERIVED] More generally, let an honest result satisfy `Pr[Y∈B]=p`, and let an adversary select an event `D` of probability `a>0` after learning the result. Then `Pr[Y∈B | D] ≤ min(1,p/a)`, by `Pr[Y∈B ∩ D] ≤ p`. For a fair bit and a half-available service, perfect bias among delivered outputs is possible. The exact witness above attains it. A throughput or availability promise is therefore mathematically relevant to conditional-release fairness; integrity alone cannot supply it.

[DERIVED] “Unbiased” must mean the prescribed distribution of `Step(state,command;r)` conditional on the admitted state and command. It cannot mean that authorized observations or commands have no effect on the resident. Legitimate learning changes behavior. The adversarial issue here is choosing internal coins or selecting hidden alternative executions outside the admitted interface.

## Minimal additional functionality for the stronger release claim

[DERIVED specification] A sufficient ideal boundary is an **irreversible admission before outcome information, followed by outcome-independent completion/publication**. These words identify responsibilities; they are not names for a supplied cryptographic implementation:

1. [DERIVED] The continuity authority validates and atomically reserves the exact `(genesis,parent,slot,command,recipient,program/version)` before the host obtains information correlated with the candidate's development coins or output. Admission binds the semantic command, not caller-chosen encryption randomness or an arbitrary nonce. It must include the authorized source of any private observation without making that observation public.
2. [DERIVED] A protected mechanism assigns one sample to that reservation. The prescribed conditional distribution must be unknown to the selecting host at the admission cut. Retry, proof failure, restored manifests and alternate ciphertext encodings cannot request a new sample. A receipt binds the reservation, coin commitment, executed private relation, successor and output packet. A proof of consistency alone does not establish the sampling distribution.
3. [DERIVED] Once an output-correlated fact becomes available to the host, the corresponding successor/output has already entered the immutable accounting history, or an independent mechanism can complete and publish precisely that result. Final publication must make the addressed packet retrievable without depending on this host's continued goodwill. A host may copy its own received messages; the property concerns fresh authorized results.
4. [DERIVED] If only the first two clauses are realized, claim **unique admitted randomness with possible selective noncompletion**, not unbiased completed outputs. If outputs remain hidden from the host and every prepublication leakage channel is independent of them, a host can abort without choosing based on the outcome; this alternative relies on that leakage theorem. Arbitrary network delivery cannot be forced in the threat model where every delivery path is controlled by the adversary.

[DERIVED limitation] This boundary has no need to give the continuity authority a state-decryption key. But the restricted sampler/evaluator must actually protect its implementation under the claimed exposure tier, and the completion path must actually be available. These are exactly the unresolved capabilities, not consequences of adding fields to a receipt. A beacon alone supplies neither.

[DERIVED] Coins sampled during honest initialization can support a bounded random-function ideal: each history has one hidden predetermined continuation, repeats reuse it, and distinct histories have independent coins. This is different from entropy arriving after setup. It can suffice for computational unpredictability while the seed/table remains protected. A forkable evaluator may let its operator inspect all permitted branch outputs and choose a final branch; such selection is consistent with a forkable ideal. Preventing it is a stronger interface/continuity requirement. The transition-tree lane should retain this distinction instead of labeling its setup coins biased merely because the final host-selected branch is biased.

[EXECUTED] A deliberately false-accepting toy gate gives each context an independent uniform four-valued oracle answer, and accepts a false receipt when that answer is zero. Each fixed context has error `1/4`. With three queries and an additional unqueried final candidate, an adversary finalizes just one context but succeeds with `175/256` over all 256 oracle tables. Counting one finalized context and retaining `1/4` fails. Counting all four opportunities gives the valid union bound 1. This is a counterexample to the inference rule “fixed-context rate survives postselection”; it is not an attack on Stage 0 and does not determine its tighter global rate.

[DERIVED / EXECUTED in Lean] The proposed `Selvage/RandomnessSelection.lean` reuses the existing `uniformProb` and union/product lemmas. It proves:

- `selection_transfer`: for an arbitrary selector depending on the sampled world, the selected bad-event probability is at most the sum of bad-event probabilities across all selectable contexts.
- `selection_transfer_uniform`: if all those probabilities are at most ε, the bound is `n*ε`. No independence among those events is assumed. The same underlying sample space must support every event.
- `fresh_after_selection`: a selector depending on prior history retains ε if a new independent coin is drawn afterward and **every conditional history/context fibre** has error at most ε.
- `coinblind_release_balanced`: selection invariant under a probability-preserving pairing of opposite Boolean outcomes preserves equal released masses. `conditional_balance` explicitly retains positive release probability.

[EXECUTED nonvacuity] The Boolean diagonal has both fixed-context probabilities `1/2` and adaptive-selected probability 1. A nonconstant history-dependent selector with an independent fresh coin satisfies the positive transfer premise. An independent history bit gives both delivered and undelivered worlds while preserving balanced outputs. Selecting delivery by the output violates blindness and balance. These are theorem-level witnesses, with no `sorry` or custom axioms.
