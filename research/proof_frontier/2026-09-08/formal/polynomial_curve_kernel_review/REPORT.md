## Mathematical review

[SOURCE statement] Source lines 53–66 separate `DegreePremises M e K` from `DegreeBoundedKernel M e K` before the proofs. For an arbitrary field `F`, finite row type `ι`, and `K : Matrix ι (Fin (e+1)) F[X]`, the premises are `e+1 ≤ card ι`, every entry's natural degree at most `M`, and vanishing determinants for every selection of `e+1` rows. The conclusion is an actual nonzero polynomial vector `a` with `K*a=0` and every coordinate's natural degree at most `M*e`. There is no finite-field, positive-`M`, positive-`e`, interpolation or point-evaluation assumption. `degree_correctness` at lines 458–461 passes exactly those premises to the generalized theorem.

[DERIVED determinant] The proof at lines 159–196 expands the determinant over permutations. Each product uses exactly `n` entries, so its natural degree is at most their degree sum, at most `n*M=M*n`. Multiplication by a permutation sign does not increase degree; the degree of a finite polynomial sum is bounded by the maximum summand bound. Cancellation and zero products can only lower the resulting degree. This proves the stated inequality, including the empty determinant, without assuming a nonzero determinant. Diagonal matrices with every diagonal entry `X^M` attain degree `M*n`; the bound itself is sharp.

[DERIVED rank construction] Lines 224–442 choose the largest size `r ≤ n=e+1` of an embedded nonzero square minor. The empty minor has determinant `1`, so the chosen set is inhabited even at rank zero. The full-minor premise excludes `r=n`, including arbitrary column embeddings because an embedding of `Fin n` into itself is a permutation; thus `r≤e`. The shape premise supplies a row outside the selected `r` rows, and `r<n` supplies a column outside the selected columns. Appending both produces a singular `(r+1)`-square matrix `B` by maximality.

[DERIVED nonzero and degree] The last adjugate column `u` of `B` is killed by `B`. Its last coordinate is a nonzero signed copy of the selected `r`-minor, so this column is nonzero. Every coordinate is a signed determinant of an `r`-square submatrix of `K`, hence has natural degree at most `M*r`. The proof extends `u` by zero along the injective selected-column map. This preserves nonzeroness and bounds the remaining coordinates by `natDegree 0=0`, giving the claimed `M*r≤M*e`; the multiplier is the cofactor size `r`, not `r+1`.

[DERIVED all rows] Selected rows annihilate the extended vector by the original adjugate equation. For any other row, replacing the appended row gives another `(r+1)`-minor, again singular by maximality. Laplace expansion along the replaced row uses the same adjugate column, because those cofactors omit that row. Its determinant equation therefore gives the missing row's annihilation. This checks the extension to every row, including tall matrices, rather than just the selected square block. Allowing all row functions in the premise is harmless: repeated-row determinants vanish, and injective row choices supply the actual maximal minors.

[DERIVED boundaries] All of the following are consistent with the source proof and the natural-degree convention:

- `M=0`: entries are constant polynomials. Every cofactor has degree zero, so the conclusion gives a constant nonzero vector. The proof uses non-strict multiplication monotonicity and never divides by `M`.
- `e=0`: there is one column and at least one row. Every entry is a selected `1×1` determinant, so the premise forces `K=0`. The vector `(1)` has degree `0=M*0` and is nonzero. A nonzero matrix satisfying this boundary premise cannot exist; the actual kernel conclusion remains inhabited.
- `r=0`: the selected minor is the empty determinant `1`; `B` is the zero `1×1` matrix and its adjugate is `(1)`. Thus the proof's nonzero cofactor survives this boundary.
- `n=0` in the separate determinant helper: `det([])=1`, whose natural degree is `0=M*0`.
- Characteristic two: signs become `1`, still nonzero. Neither the cofactor argument nor the witnesses require `1≠-1`.

[SOURCE/DERIVED witnesses] Lines 548–590 give, for every `M`, the nonzero matrix with both rows `[X^M,1]` and nonzero annihilator `[1,-X^M]`. The entry `1` witnesses matrix nonzeroness even at `M=0`. The two rows have every maximal minor zero, and the vector has maximum degree `M`. `degree_premises_inhabited` and `degree_satisfiable` therefore establish nonvacuity over every field for `e=1`, including genuinely higher-degree entries when `M>1`.

[SOURCE/DERIVED falsifiers] The identity matrix at lines 593–602 meets the size and degree assumptions for every `M,e`, but admits no nonzero kernel, so the vanishing-minor premise has content. The strict-degree theorem at lines 606–628 uses `X^M*a₀+a₁=0`: if `a₀≠0`, then `natDegree a₁=M+natDegree a₀≥M`; otherwise `a₁=0` as well. Thus no nonzero annihilator can have all coordinate degrees below `M`. This establishes sharpness at `e=1` for positive `M`. At `M=0`, the strict condition `natDegree a_t<0` is itself impossible; that boundary is not an additional nontrivial sharpness claim. Lines 631–635 separately show that weakening the entry-degree premise to a falsely substituted `M=0` conclusion fails already for the old `[X,1]` matrix.

[DERIVED additional public witness] The independent checker also uses an `(e+1)`-square matrix with `e` rows expressing `X^M*a_i+a_(i+1)=0` and one zero row. The first `e` rows and last `e` columns have determinant `1`; its rank is exactly `e`. The vector `a_j=(-X^M)^j` is nonzero and attains maximum degree `M*e`. For any nonzero kernel vector, the recurrence gives `a_j=(-X^M)^j*a₀` and forces `a₀≠0`, so its last coordinate has degree `M*e+natDegree a₀`. This also shows general `M*e` sharpness when `M,e>0`.
