## Threat model
When SHE's cryptography holds, the attacker goes above and around it: (1) **wetware**: an untrusted AR grain emits imperceptible flicker or strobe, or a covert channel in RGBA/audio LSBs or frame timing; (2) **coercion**: the wearer is forced to unlock, and refusing, or a variable slot count, gives away a hidden compartment. `[DERIVED]` statements below are Lean theorems in the file above, cited by name.

## Part A — `WetwareSafety` scrubber (per byte channel: quantize → slew clamp → reversal hold-off)
- `[DERIVED]` `scrubber_enforces_slew_and_reversal_bound`: for **every** raw input stream, the emitted trace satisfies `|o(t+1) − o(t)| ≤ dmax`, and any two luminance reversals are `≥ H` frames apart. A reversal (`revAt`) is defined by an *observer* from the emitted trace alone (`odir` = direction of the last nonzero change), not from the guard's state.
- `[DERIVED]` `revSpaced_count_le`: at most `n` reversals in any `n·H`-frame window. `wearable60_at_most_5_reversals_per_second` (`Q=4, dmax=16, H=12` at 60 fps): ≤ 5 reversals in any 60-frame window, i.e. ≤ 2.5 flash pairs/s. That is under the ITU-R BT.1702 limit `[REPORTED: web-search summary of BT.1702, not read here]`: a flash is a pair of opposing luminance changes, and no more than 3 flashes in any one second. So **no flash train above 3 Hz can leave the lens, whatever the grain sends** `[DERIVED + REPORTED threshold]`. The guard is per pixel, so it does not rely on BT.1702's area exemption.
- `[DERIVED]` `scrubber_kills_subthreshold_stego`: two raw A/V streams that agree on the video high bits (`x/Q`), the audio high bits (`s/2^k`) and the cadence bucket (`τ/P`) produce the **identical** scrubbed stream (video, audio, release ticks). `scrubber_zero_covert_capacity`: the scrubbed stream factors through that perceptual projection (`∃ F, scrubAV s = F (highBits s)`), so an LSB or sub-tick jitter channel through the lens carries **0 bits/frame** (a noninterference statement). Channels left in the high bits (slow visible patterns) are not covered.
- `[DERIVED]` `snapRelease_bounds` / `snapRelease_kills_jitter`: release at the next vsync boundary (`τ < snap ≤ τ+P`, `P ∣ snap`); any jitter `j < P` maps to the same release tick.
- `[DERIVED]` `scrubber_fixpoint_on_safe_canonical` (a safe, lattice, byte input passes unchanged) and `scrubber_idempotent_on_safe_canonical` (scrubbing the output again changes nothing; needs `scrubber_output_is_lattice_byte`, which also justifies Rust's `u8` state).
- `[DERIVED]` `#tooth`s: `tooth_raw_15hz_strobe_not_photosafe` (raw 15 Hz strobe reverses at frames 5 and 7); `tooth_15hz_strobe_caught` (frame 7 held dark); `tooth_hold_off_load_bearing` (`H=1` lets the strobe through); `tooth_lsb_keystream_killed` (a 2-bit/frame key stream, `0xB4` vs `0x1F`: raw streams differ, scrubbed streams are identical); `tooth_no_quant_leaks` (`Q=1` leaks).
- `[DERIVED]` **Scope finding**, `tooth_per_channel_rgb_misses_luma_flicker`: two channels with no reversals, stepping in opposite directions on alternate frames, sum to a 30 Hz luma flicker. The theorem binds the channel the guard runs on, so the deployed guard must run on the **luma plane `Y'`** (plus `Cb`/`Cr`/alpha), never on `R`, `G`, `B` separately. Rust mirrors this check (`tooth_per_channel_rgb_misses_luma_flicker` test).

## Part B — `DuressChaffVolume` (fixed `N` slots, every slot one equal-size block)
- Construction: `slot_i = (body ‖ tag) ⊕ pad`, where `pad = cSHAKE256(root ‖ salt_i ‖ i)`, `tag = cSHAKE256(root ‖ salt_i ‖ i ‖ body)` (separate customizations), and `salt_i = cSHAKE256(nonce ‖ i)` is distinct for each vault and slot. Lean `SlotScheme.seal/open_` over any additive group (Rust: 64-byte XOR). Unlock tries every slot; there is no slot table.
- `[DERIVED]` `duress_adversary_cannot_distinguish_sovereign_from_chaff`: for **every** distinguisher `A` (which may embed the duress key, salts and decoy plaintexts), `#{R | A(realImage R)} = #{U | A(chaffImage U)}`. The proof is the bijection `R ↦ seal + R` (`sovShift`). `duress_view_distribution_equal`: pointwise-equal view distributions, i.e. **TVD = 0**. `sovereign_population_hidden`: any two sovereign populations (including different counts) look alike. `duress_unlock_correct`: duress unlock opens every decoy slot to its plaintext, whatever the sovereign data.
- `[DERIVED]` **Model boundary:** the theorem is in the *ideal-XOF* model, where sovereign pads are an independent uniform vector. With deployed cSHAKE256 the claim is computational. `[HYPOTHESIS]` The advantage ≤ the PRF advantage against cSHAKE256 under the sovereign root; that reduction is **not mechanised**.
- `[DERIVED]` `#tooth`s: `tooth_variable_geometry_breaks` (storing only occupied slots: a length test has advantage 1 for every pad); `tooth_shared_salt_kdf_breaks` (pad derived from the shared salt without the root: the duress holder strips it, and a header test flags the sovereign slot 4/4 vs chaff 2/4 → advantage ½).
- `[EXECUTED]` Rust: decoy/sovereign unlock round-trip; the duress view is identical with and without a sovereign root; image length is always `32 + 64·N`; `rewrite_owned` refuses slots the root does not open (`NotOwned`), so duress-side writes never clobber sovereign blocks; the broken shared-salt pad flags slots `{3, 11}` exactly, the real scheme flags none; hidden-slot byte χ² (sanity check, not a proof) = 232.6 sovereign / 247.9 chaff (255 dof, 400 vaults).

## Open / not claimed
- `[OPEN]` **Linear-light luminance:** `Y'` is gamma-encoded, non-constant-luminance luma. Chroma motion at fixed `Y'` can still move linear luminance. Running the guard on BT.2020 constant-luminance `Yc` or ICtCp `I` would close this; not done.
- `[OPEN]` BT.1702's saturated-red flash and spatial-pattern criteria are not modelled. Backlight/PWM flicker (the IEEE 1789 domain) is hardware, below the content layer, and out of scope; no IEEE 1789 number is used here.
- `[OPEN]` **Multi-snapshot adversary:** imaging the device twice shows which blocks changed. Single-snapshot deniability does not survive that unless every write re-randomizes all non-owned chaff, which needs the sovereign root to preserve its slots (TrueCrypt hidden-volume problem `[REPORTED: folklore]`).
- `[OPEN]` The passphrase KDF is one cSHAKE256 call (not memory-hard; Argon2id-class needed). The chaff source is caller-supplied (production: OS CSPRNG). Deniability does not stop a coercer who knows the scheme from demanding "the other key". That is a limit of the deniable-encryption game, not of this construction `[HYPOTHESIS]`.
- `[OPEN]` The JSON `vault_regression` digest is Rust-pinned (self-generated), not Lean-authored. The scrubber vectors are Lean-authored.
